# Personal airfield passports for friends

An airfield passport is a collection of places you want to visit, with a map
that fills as you record your arrivals. Friends using a passport-only account
can now create and edit their own collections, add missing airfields and prepare
a linked next edition. Their military and ULM labels apply to their own passport;
public airfield details keep other collections’ private corrections separate.

Linked group editions now compare coverage by anyone, by everyone and by each
participant, alongside the airfields added or removed. Each edition has its own
percentage, with read-only history clearly separated from participation. Older
shared maps also open reliably while a newer edition is active.

**Where next?** helps those friends find unvisited destinations using public
airfield data and fields shared through their passports. They can enter speed
and range estimates manually, with access checked against their current
passport permissions.

Manual visits can now be corrected in place: change the date or note, or
replace/remove a supporting flight. Corrections update personal progress while
preserving the flight logbook.

**Export my passport data** downloads a ZIP with your visits and notes, voucher
allowances and use, current completion and stamp photos. Retained personal
records remain exportable after leaving a group; its later private changes and
other friends’ evidence stay out of the download.

Aviation labels are clearer in French and Dutch. Passport completion, remaining
vouchers and the aircraft’s main photo use distinct terms. Flight forms explicitly
label landing times, destination searches identify their reference airfield,
and group membership status is distinguished from the action to leave. Visited
airfields and validity dates use consistent wording across pages.
